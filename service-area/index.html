The places we work most.

Each of these has its own housing stock, and its own version of the same job.

HAMILTON COUNTY

Carmel

Subdivision stock from the 1980s to the 2000s, now at the age where sealed units fail rather than just look tired.

 HAMILTON COUNTY

Fishers

Built fast between 1995 and 2010, which means whole streets reach replacement age in the same season.

 HAMILTON COUNTY

Noblesville

Two housing stocks in one city: historic Old Town openings, and newer subdivisions on the edges.

 BOONE COUNTY

Zionsville

Where the grille pattern does most of the visual work, and the wrong one is what people notice.

 HAMILTON COUNTY

Westfield

Much of it genuinely new — so a good share of the work here is on houses under twenty years old.

 JOHNSON COUNTY

Greenwood

Post-war ranches and split-levels, and most of the aluminium frames we still take out.

And the rest of it.

Same crews, same warranty, same measure-before-quoting.

MARION COUNTY

Indianapolis

Every township, from Broad Ripple bungalows to the newer additions on the north and west sides.

HENDRICKS COUNTY

Avon

West-side subdivisions, most of them built from the late 1990s onward.

HENDRICKS COUNTY

Brownsburg

A mix of older homes near the town centre and newer building to the north.

HENDRICKS COUNTY

Plainfield

Post-war housing along the old National Road, plus newer growth south of it.

TIPPECANOE COUNTY

Lafayette

Including West Lafayette. Older housing stock and a long drive we still make.

MADISON COUNTY

Anderson

Substantial pre-war and mid-century housing, much of it with original openings.

DELAWARE COUNTY

Muncie

Older brick homes and rentals where full-frame replacement is often the honest answer.

HOWARD COUNTY

Kokomo

Mid-century ranches and the newer additions around them.

BARTHOLOMEW COUNTY

Columbus

A town that takes its architecture seriously, which makes matching a sightline more of the job here than it is most places.

JACKSON COUNTY

Seymour

Halfway down I‑65, and an easy stop for a crew already heading to the river.

MONROE COUNTY

Bloomington

Limestone-era homes near the centre, fast newer building east of it, and a lot of rental stock in between.

CLARK COUNTY

Jeffersonville

River-town streets still carrying their original openings, with newer subdivisions pushing north from them.

CLARK COUNTY

Clarksville

Post-war ranches and mid-century additions, most of them still on the windows they were built with.

FLOYD COUNTY

New Albany

Some of the oldest housing we work in. Tall, narrow openings where a stock size is rarely the honest answer.

HARRISON COUNTY

Corydon

The old state capital, with the housing stock that goes with it, plus newer building out along the highway.

JEFFERSON COUNTY, KENTUCKY

Louisville

Across the river, and the only place we work outside Indiana. Brick homes with openings that predate any standard size.
