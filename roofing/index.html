Roof shapes

Six roof shapes, and where the water goes.

Whatever shape is on your house, a leak almost always starts where water slows down or changes direction. Here is how each common shape moves it off the roof.

  • Gable

    Two slopes meeting at a ridge, with a triangle of wall at each end. The shape most people draw when they draw a house.

    Water runs: two ways, off the long sides

  • Hip

    Slopes on every side, meeting at a short ridge. No gable walls for the wind to catch, and the gutters run all the way around.

    Water runs: all four sides

  • Gambrel

    Two pitches on each side, shallow above and steep below: the barn roof. The steep lower slope makes room for a full upstairs.

    Water runs: two ways, picking up speed on the steep lower pitch

  • Mansard

    Almost vertical lower slopes, often with dormers, under a low top. A whole top floor tucked inside the roof.

    Water runs: off a low top, then down the near-vertical sides

  • Shed

    A single slope from a high side to a low one. Common on additions, porches and modern houses.

    Water runs: one way, to a single gutter

  • Flat / low-slope

    Never truly flat: a slight pitch carries water to a drain or scupper. Covered with a continuous membrane rather than shingles.

    Water runs: gently, to a drain or scupper
